# International compliance screening matrix

**Status:** Screening only; counsel must confirm applicability and country list before launch. The source supports store-country pricing; no active rollout country set was verified.

| Market / framework | Why it may matter | Main topics to assess | Required owner evidence / action |
|---|---|---|---|
| India: DPDP Act 2023 and 2025 Rules | Indian company and India operations. Rules and Act have staged commencement; actual obligations and dates require current Gazette review. | Notice, consent/other permitted grounds, data principal rights, security safeguards, breach notices, processor contracts, children, erasure, grievance contact. | Counsel to map provisions to effective dates; owner approve purposes/grounds, age policy, grievance role, processor contracts and retention. |
| India: consumer and e-commerce rules | Paid digital subscriptions offered to Indian consumers may be in scope depending on business model. | Seller disclosures, grievance process, cancellation/refund rights, clear price and renewal information, fair marketing. | Counsel assess entity classification and designate/display required grievance contact if applicable; finance approve GST/accounting treatment. |
| EEA: GDPR and consumer/e-commerce rules | Could apply if services are offered to or behavior monitored for people in EEA. | Lawful basis, transparent notice, access/erasure/objection, processor terms, transfer mechanism, security/breach response, child consent threshold, digital-content withdrawal/consumer rights. | Confirm launch countries/users; counsel assess territorial scope, representative/DPO thresholds, transfers, consumer flow and local rules. |
| UK: UK GDPR / Data Protection Act and consumer law | Could apply if service is offered to UK consumers or monitors them. UK DMCC subscription rules have staged commencement and current guidance; check launch-date commencement. | Privacy rights, international transfers, subscription pre-contract information, reminders, cooling-off/cancellation, digital content rights. | UK counsel assess current statutory commencement and app-store intermediation, cancellation, notices and refund flow. |
| United States: state privacy laws | Applicability varies by state, business thresholds, processing and exemptions. | Notices, rights, sensitive data, targeted ads/sharing, opt-out signals, data minimization and retention. | Counsel confirm states, thresholds, whether data sales/sharing or targeted advertising occur; no dedicated ad SDK identified in source but production must be checked. |
| United States: COPPA | Relevant if child-directed or actual knowledge of collecting personal information from under-13 children. | Audience assessment, parental notice/consent, rights, data minimization/security/retention. | Owner decide intended audience; counsel review child appeal and marketing. Do not declare “not for children” without product and store audience confirmation. |
| Other countries | Store pricing/country configuration is not proof of current launch. Local privacy, consumer, subscription, tax, accessibility, language, content and age rules may differ. | Privacy, local consumer rights, auto-renewal disclosures, tax/VAT, refunds, content/licensing, cross-border transfer. | Business owner supply launch-country list and store availability; counsel/tax professional assess each market. |

## Screening principles

- No inference that a country is targeted solely from website accessibility, country dropdown support, users appearing in logs, or an available store territory.
- App stores control payment instrument, checkout localization and some customer-facing subscription flows; they do not eliminate the operator's privacy, content, consumer or deletion duties.
- Map every SDK and server-side provider—including hosting, database, mail, media, push, store APIs, diagnostics and support—to data, purpose, region, contract, retention and deletion evidence.
- This matrix is not a complete jurisdictional legal survey and does not substitute for local counsel.
